# Clipping other creators' streams legally (researched 2026-11-25)

Overlord publishes third-party content only when the job carries a rights basis **and** a reference (campaign link or
written permission). Without the reference, clips are rendered but never uploaded.

## Where permission comes from
| Route | How it works | Pay (reported, varies by campaign) |
|---|---|---|
| Clipping marketplaces (Whop Content Rewards, Clipping.net/.io, Vyro, Ssemble) | Join a creator's or brand's campaign, follow its posting rules, submit links; views are verified | about $0.50–$3 per 1,000 views; per-clip caps common |
| Creator-run programmes | Streamer pays clippers directly (e.g. Kick streamer N3on: $40 per 100k views) | set by the streamer |
| Brand campaigns around a creator | e.g. an Expedia × IShowSpeed campaign on Content Rewards paid $1 per 1,000 views, $10 minimum, $500 cap per clip | set by the brand |
| Kick's own clipping programme | Sources disagree whether clippers can apply directly or only streamers; check Kick's Discord | not published |

Practical rules: use the campaign's own tags/links, respect per-clip caps, never buy views (campaigns reject botted
views), keep the campaign URL in the job's rights reference.

## Example: IShowSpeed GTA 6 stream (youtu.be/M2qGeO7OvHM)
Uploaded by "Live Speedy", 11h58m, `licensedContent: true` (Content ID). Not clippable to the owner's channel without
joining an active IShowSpeed campaign; mass re-uploads risk claims and strikes (three strikes terminate a channel).
